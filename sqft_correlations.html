Observations

What the data is saying

Heights $/sqft is not a standalone series. It is the local price of scarce inner-loop housing, set against metro jobs, borrowing costs, and household purchasing power. The charts below overlay those series so the relationship is visible — not inferred from a single median sale price.

  • Appreciation is structural, not a one-cycle spike. Median sold $/sqft rose from about $87 in 1997 to $410 in 2025. The steepest run was 2012–2022; 2023–24 flattened, then 2025 printed a new high.
  • Jobs and income support the bid. Houston MSA employment remains in a long expansion near 3.5 million. Harris County median household income has climbed into the mid-$70ks, which underwrites higher inner-loop price per foot.
  • Inventory is the local amplifier. Heights months of supply has often run tighter than the broader Houston market. Historic-district rules and limited lots mean positive demand shows up in $/sqft faster than in outer-ring product.
  • Rates remain the swing factor. The 2018 and 2022–23 hikes coincide with slower $/sqft gains. Rate relief has historically lined up with faster appreciation.
  • Schools add a family premium, not a uniform one. Field and Sinclair elementary score well above HISD averages; Heights High is solid. Nearby private campuses (A / A+ on Niche) expand the buyer pool that will pay for location.

Detail

Key observations, expanded

Employment

Houston MSA nonfarm / household employment has recovered from the 2020 air pocket and sits near 3.5 million. Inner-loop neighborhoods capture a share of that job base through commuting convenience and amenities. Employment is a demand foundation, not a precise one-for-one predictor of Heights $/sqft in any given year.

Inventory

Months of supply in the Heights cluster has often been tighter than the metro. That tightness is the mechanism: when jobs and income improve, limited listings bid up price per foot. When metro inventory floods, Heights still has a historic-district floor that outer suburbs do not.

Mortgage rates

Dual-axis overlays show slower $/sqft growth around the 2018 and 2022–23 rate spikes. Correlation is directional and lagged. Cash and equity-rich buyers in the Heights can mute rate sensitivity relative to first-time outer-ring buyers, but they do not eliminate it.

Income and businesses

Harris County SAIPE median household income rose into the mid-$70ks by 2024. Private establishments have expanded over the same long cycle. Both series describe purchasing power and local economic density. They are county-level, so they overstate how tightly they map onto a few Heights blocks.

Schools

Public quality is mixed-to-solid: Field and Sinclair elementary are local anchors; Heights High is above many HISD campuses but not a magnet-tier print. High-rated private options within a short drive support family demand that might otherwise leave for suburban districts. School ratings here are a snapshot, not a time series — they explain willingness to pay more than they explain year-to-year $/sqft moves.

Disclosures

How to read this brief

  • Research and analytics only. This is not investment advice, a solicitation, or a recommendation to buy or sell any property.
  • Several overlays (employment, months of supply, establishments) are research composites aligned to published levels. They are directionally consistent with FRED, HAR, Redfin, and Census prints, not audited restatements of every monthly observation.
  • Neighborhood $/sqft is mix-dependent. A year heavy with new-build closings can lift the median even if bungalow pricing is flat.
  • Harris County and Houston MSA series are wider than the Heights. Use them as context, not as a substitute for block-level comps.
  • School ratings are simplified onto a 0–10 scale from GreatSchools / SchoolDigger / Niche snapshots and can change with TEA accountability cycles.
  • Past appreciation is not a forecast. Rate, inventory, and employment regimes can reverse.
