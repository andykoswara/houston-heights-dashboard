Key investor observations

• Employment: Houston MSA nonfarm employment remains in long-term expansion (~3.5M). Job growth is a foundational demand driver for inner-loop neighborhoods.

• Inventory: Heights months-of-supply has stayed tighter than the broader Houston market for extended periods. Constrained supply (historic districts + limited new lots) amplifies the impact of positive employment and income trends on $/sqft.

• Schools: Public options in the core Heights (Travis / Field / Sinclair elementary, Heights High) are generally stronger than district averages and act as a local demand magnet. High-quality private schools within commuting distance further support family buyer willingness to pay a premium.

• Rates: Still the primary short-term swing factor. Rate declines have historically coincided with faster $/sqft appreciation in the neighborhood.

Correlations are directional. Local supply inelasticity means favorable macro conditions (jobs + income + lower rates) tend to show up more clearly in Heights $/sqft than in outer-ring markets.
